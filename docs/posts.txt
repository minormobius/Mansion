8. [MAJOR] Stanford's tech transfer office basically invented the modern model in 1970. $2B+ cumulative licensing revenue. Genentech in '76, Guardant Health recently. Every university copies their playbook now.

9. [MAJOR] NIH SBIR grants are the quiet engine of academic biotech. $275K Phase I, $1M+ Phase II - non-dilutive funding that lets professor-founders de-risk before VCs. Amgen, Genzyme, Geron all started this way. Government money seeding private innovation.

10. [MAJOR] Arch Venture Partners treats universities as outsourced R&D. They funded Illumina from Tufts research, Juno from Fred Hutch, Grail. Managing partner Nelsen: universities are 'the best R&D departments in the world.' VCs as academic talent scouts.

12. [MAJOR] Regeneron's bet: keep academic culture inside the company. 30%+ of revenue to R&D, discover internally vs. license. Eylea, Dupixent, COVID antibodies followed. $75B market cap validates the science-first model. Counter-example to pure spinout plays.

13. [MAJOR] HHMI's model: fund people not projects, let them take risks. $200M+/year. Doudna (CRISPR), David Liu (base editing), Feng Zhang - all HHMI investigators. Patient capital for high-risk academic science that spawns industries.
